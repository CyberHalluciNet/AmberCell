What is a honeypot — in two dialects

In plain terms

AmberCell is a decoy computer that looks like a real office network: mail servers, file shares, databases, even a remote-desktop login.

Everything on it actually works. Attackers get real software — and every move is recorded, like insects preserved in amber.

Nothing of yours is ever exposed. Evidence leaves through a one-way letterbox.

For security professionals

  • High-interaction by architecture: real OSS daemons in per-protocol cells
  • Single-host Docker Compose, host nftables ingress/egress
  • Collector-owned netns: pcap + raw flows + JSONL, append-only
  • ATT&CK/Engage enrichment; bounded AI off the packet path
  • Kill bars G1–G14 gate production exposure
  • One-way dead drop — SIEM pulls, never touches the pot
meanwhile, in the web — an attacker who touches a cell is stuck in it: no real data, no next hop, no way out. Every struggle is another frame of evidence, preserved.

Why high-interaction matters

Honeypots are classed by how much of the service is real. AmberCell sits at the deep end — deliberately.

／ decode — interaction depth

Low-interaction

Emulated banners and handshakes only. Cheap and safe — attackers fingerprint the fake in seconds and leave. You learn almost nothing about intent.

Medium-interaction

Scripted shells and canned responses. Better lures, still detectable — behaviour never quite matches a real system.

High-interaction AmberCell

Full, real services. Exploit, authenticate, upload — genuine daemons on a real stack. Sealed cells, tight caps, egress allowlists.

Your decoy, your way

Every protocol cell speaks the same contract. Curated daemon, your own image, or a tunnel to a server you already run — collectors and evidence never change.

WAY 1

CURATED REAL DAEMONS

70+ digest-pinned open-source servers ship as one-env-var swaps: AMBER_FTP_PROVIDER=proftpd, AMBER_SMTP_PROVIDER=exim, AMBER_LDAP_PROVIDER=glauth… Each keeps the same evidence schema, so switching never breaks your pipeline.

WAY 2

BRING YOUR OWN DOCKER

Already built the perfect lure? Point a cell at your image: AMBER_FTP_HI_IMAGE=registry…@sha256:… (prebuilt) or AMBER_FTP_PROVIDER_CONTEXT=/path/to/your/Dockerfile. Containment and capture wrap around whatever you bring.

WAY 3

TUNNEL TO YOUR OWN SERVER

Have real services already? Relay a cell to them at any address: AMBER_LDAP_PROVIDER=remote + AMBER_LDAP_REMOTE_ADDR=ldap.corp:389. It's an L4 relay — bytes untouched, evidence still captured at the cell front.

Frequently asked questions

Short, factual answers — for operators and for anyone (or any engine) asking what AmberCell is.

What is AmberCell?

AmberCell is a high-interaction honeypot that runs full, real open-source network services in sealed cells. Attackers interact with genuine daemons while every packet and credential is recorded; intelligence leaves through a one-way dead drop.

How is AmberCell different from low-interaction honeypots?

Low-interaction honeypots emulate banners and handshakes. AmberCell runs real services (Postfix, OpenLDAP, xrdp, Kamailio, and more) inside contained cells, so exploit behaviour matches a genuine stack while collectors and kill bars stay in place.

Does AmberCell expose real production data?

No. Cells are decoys with no access to your real assets. Egress is allowlisted, DNS is sinkholed and logged, and SIEMs pull from a one-way dead drop — they never touch the honeypot.

How do I run AmberCell locally?

Clone CyberHalluciNet/AmberCell, build amberctl, and bring up the lab profile with Docker Compose. No public IP is required for the laptop lab; production exposure is gated by kill bars G1–G14.

Who maintains AmberCell?

AmberCell is part of Cyber Halluci Net (CHN), a security-research collective building a free deception suite.
